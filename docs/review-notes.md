# Source contribution: review handoff

This file records builder findings and assumptions. It is not the separate independent review and does not attest or authorize deployment. Policy ownership and signed artifact linkage belong to launch services. A reviewer should examine source and the eventual manifest together.

## Concrete requirement conflicts

1. **EIP-1167 clones versus no proxies/delegatecall.** The workflow asks for a clone per loan and also says no proxies; the launch guidance forbids DELEGATECALL, CALLCODE and SELFDESTRUCT. This implementation uses a standalone CollateralVault per loan, created and initialized atomically by PawnShop. That preserves isolation, one-time initialization and custody restrictions, but costs more gas and does not implement the requested EIP-1167 deployment form. Treat this as a documented source/requirement deviation. All deployed runtime types are tested with the protected opcode-walking algorithm.
2. **Pinned canonical question hashes versus changing windows.** The supplied oracle definition says `questionHash` covers the resolved block window and definitions. A fresh request with a relative window therefore need not have yesterday's hash. PawnShop implements exact hash matching, one-time initial configuration, and delayed collection/hash updates; MilestoneBurn allows one initial hash only. Neither silently weakens the signed question check. Continuous daily pricing requires an oracle-supported canonical question/window policy compatible with those pins; this is not established by a local mock. The operator and independent reviewer must resolve this before enabling borrowing or funding the burn vault. Do not describe a freshly signed answer about an old historical window as a current price.
3. **Optional zero attester versus canonical nonzero signer.** The supplied real attester is known and was confirmed by the public API. Constructors require it as a nonzero input, following the unmodified canonical consumer. The workflow's optional `set a zero attester once` path is consequently not exposed. PawnShop can rotate the signer after 48 hours. MilestoneBurn now mirrors PawnShop's governed signer through permissionless `syncSigner()` and synchronizes on every burn. Its additional constructor argument is `$contract:PawnShop`; it has no ongoing owner or token withdrawal.
4. **Application deployment graph.** PawnShop's constructor creates LendingPool and LockDiscount with its own real address, avoiding forward/circular constructor references and post-deployment initialization calls. Vaults are created on demand. The manifest deploys the token, PawnShop, and then MilestoneBurn (whose fourth constructor argument now references PawnShop); it must not add duplicate pool/module/vault instances. Review and publish child source and constructor arguments too. This source contribution does not generate `launch.json`.

## Chosen defaults and trust boundaries

- All percentages are bps. Only two term IDs exist; both term choices are snapshotted per loan. Late extension uses `max(now, due) + duration` and is allowed until auction start. Module swaps affect future loans only; extensions and release use the loan's original module. Fees cannot exceed the saved undiscounted term fee.
- The owner can classify newly queued collections as seats. That designation controls the higher concentration limit and worker authorization. Review each collection's actual behavior; ERC-721 callbacks do not establish the economic validity of collateral.
- Floor freshness is bounded by both 26 hours and the attestation's actual expiry. Floor submissions require a signed lifetime of at least 26 hours, preventing short-lived answers from shortening effective freshness without extending a signature. A long expiry does not prevent a later ordinary daily update. Newer means strictly later `issuedAt`; two same-timestamp attestations cannot both update a collection. A changed question invalidates lending freshness while preserving the last price for auctions.
- Exact registered worker hashes alone return ERC-1271 magic. The domain verifies against the NFT collection, and the message wallet is the vault, as the seat's owner. This grants no general-purpose contract signature authority. Check the live service's ERC-1271 support, enrollment lifetime and ownership checks with an actual seat before launch; tests prove the Solidity predicate, not off-chain service behavior.
- Bounties skip on insufficient balance; the valid update still consumes its 24-hour interval. No keeper action depends on payment availability. Bounty top-ups are irrevocable.
- Pool donations, loan fees and late recoveries are independent seven-day linear streams represented by cumulative checkpoints. Donations require nonzero actual share supply; income at zero shares and residual streams at the last-share exit are reclassified into reserve. Dust cannot reset a stream or fill a fixed queue. Direct WETH transfers are immediate share income, and forced ETH is excluded. Pool shares have 24 decimals.
- MilestoneBurn's immutable question setter is the original policy owner, not PawnShop's subsequent owner. This one-time configuration role cannot move funds. A sink transfer leaves totalSupply unchanged and any later transfers to the burn vault are unrecoverable.
- The generic instruction "Ownable2Step everywhere" is applied to contracts with the listed administrative powers. Adding ownership to the explicitly ownerless lock or burn vault would widen custody/control.
- A claim is a withdrawal of the caller's recorded credit; the caller may choose a receiving address. No administrator can claim someone else's credit. Failed sends revert without deleting the credit.

## Review focus

Review oracle price semantics and market-cap manipulation resistance, module swap and release behavior, NFT collection trust, timelock authority, outstanding-principal valuation, reserve segregation, native/WETH accounting, and all privileged constructor arguments. Verify the manifest `$owner` resolves to the approved owner and WETH/signer match the chain. No owner rescue paths, delegate calls, funded wallets or broadcasts are present.

The provided protected tests require deployment-service environment values and a finished manifest; their environment-dependent harness is not imported into ordinary tests. The repository has local equivalents for supply, untouched constructor allocation, bytecode size and forbidden opcodes. The services remain responsible for their own independent protected checks, source verification, attestation and deployment.

The revision responses are in `.imd-responses.json`. The certain-loss and missing-collateral proofs failed before the changes and passed after them. Expected auction losses are marked at auction start, can be refreshed permissionlessly, and settle against reserves without double counting. Unsold auctions can be written off after 40 days without conveying the NFT; late recoveries accrue to current holders. This is checkpointed book accounting, not continuous repricing: keepers must refresh declining-price allowances and write off overdue auctions before additional losses enter the share price. Tests preserve that operational boundary explicitly.

The question/window conflict in item 2 remains a disputed scope item, not a claim of oracle compatibility. A locally signed fresh-window hash is correctly rejected by the specified exact pin. No evidence of a stable live standing-question hash was obtained in this revision. Changing to immediate owner-approved hashes or adding request/callback correlation would change the approved one-shot/delayed question authority and requires an explicit protocol-compatible policy. Leave borrowing paused and do not fund the burn vault while this remains unresolved.

Foundry unit, fuzz and stateful invariant checks are run locally. Slither, Mythril, a mainnet fork, external service pairing, and an independent adversarial review have not been performed by this contribution. Those omissions are not represented as successful checks.
